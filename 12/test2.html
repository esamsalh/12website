Play the Falling Sand Game Online

Falling Sand Game is a free online sand simulator built around real-time particle movement, temperature, density and material reactions.

Pour sand into piles, carve channels for water, grow plants in wet soil, ignite wood and oil, freeze liquids, cool lava into stone or heat sand until it becomes glass. Every pixel behaves as an individual particle, so small changes can create completely different outcomes.

This browser-based falling sand simulator combines the freedom of a creative sandbox with the unpredictability of a physics game. There are no fixed goals in Open Sandbox mode. You can build a landscape, design a laboratory, test element reactions or simply watch a world develop on its own.

Why this sand physics game feels different

  • ✓Density-based movement lets oil float, water flow and heavy powders sink.
  • ✓Heat travels between particles and triggers melting, boiling, burning and cooling.
  • ✓Plants grow near moisture, fire spreads through fuel and acid corrodes materials.
  • ✓Works directly in desktop and mobile browsers with no installation required.

Frequently asked questions

Falling Sand Game FAQ

What is a falling sand game?

It is a sandbox game built from thousands of small particles on a two-dimensional grid. Every particle follows rules for gravity, density, temperature and reactions, producing complex behavior from simple interactions.

Is this Falling Sand Game free?

Yes. The complete game is free to play in your browser. It does not require an account, download or installation.

Can I play the sand simulator on a phone?

Yes. The canvas and controls adapt to mobile screens, and you can draw particles using touch gestures.

Which elements are available?

The game includes sand, water, salt, soil, oil, acid, lava, ice, fire, smoke, steam, wood, plants, seeds, stone, glass, metal, gunpowder and sparks.

Why does water turn into steam?

The simulation tracks approximate particle temperature. Water becomes steam when heated, while steam can cool and condense back into water.

Can I save or share a creation?

You can save one simulation in local browser storage, export the canvas as an image and use the share button to send the page to others.
