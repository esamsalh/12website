Original simulation engine

How this falling sand online world behaves

The world is divided into thousands of small cells. Each cell stores a material, lifespan and reaction state. During every simulation step, the engine processes cells in alternating directions to reduce unnatural bias. It then renders only simple pixel data and a lightweight bird layer, helping keep the falling sands fast across desktop and mobile devices.

Powder rules

Powders try to move down first, then diagonally. Dense grains can sink through liquids and produce natural-looking slopes.

Liquid rules

Liquids search downward, diagonally and sideways. Density allows water to settle below oil while lava moves more slowly.

Heat and reactions

Fire burns wood, leaves, oil and powder. Water cools lava into rock, steam rises and intense heat turns sand into glass.

Wildlife response

Birds are separate lightweight entities. They sample nearby cells, avoid threats, struggle in water and lose flight when overwhelmed.

Frequently asked questions

Falling sand game questions

What is a falling sand game?

It is a cellular simulation where each small cell represents a material. Simple local rules make sand fall, water flow, smoke rise, plants grow and fire spread.

Is the game free and unblocked?

The game is free and requires no account. Whether a website is accessible on a particular network depends on that network's filtering rules.

Why do the birds react to falling materials?

Every bird samples the nearby particle grid. Heavy debris, deep water, acid, fire and lava influence its direction, speed and health.

Does this version contain people, stick figures or zombies?

No. Those phrases are covered as future dedicated editions in the page content. The current playable sandbox contains natural materials and birds only.

Can the game work on a phone?

Yes. Touch drawing is supported, and the internal grid automatically uses a practical resolution for the available screen.
